# What Actually Correlates With Church Attendance Growth: Verified, Quantified Findings

*Compiled 2026-11-05. Written with a Lutheran congregation in mind (ELCA, LCMS or WELS), but the data covers U.S. congregations in general.*

Every number below was checked against the primary source: the original report PDF, the survey topline, or the researcher's own write-up. Where I read a value off a bar chart rather than from the text, I mark it with "≈". Secondary blog claims that I couldn't verify have been left out.

---

## 1. The short answer

The best single dataset on this is **Faith Communities Today (FACT)**, run by the Hartford Institute for Religion Research. It is a repeated national survey of U.S. congregations (11,077 in 2010; 15,278 in 2020). The 2010 wave was analyzed specifically for growth, both one factor at a time and with multivariate controls, in C. Kirk Hadaway's *FACTs on Growth: 2010*.

Hadaway ran a multivariate analysis that controls for location, size and age structure. The factors **a congregation can control** that still predicted growth after those controls were:

| Rank | Factor | Effect size (share of congregations in the top growth tier, 2005–2010) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Members personally involved in recruiting and inviting.** Called "the strongest relationship of all" apart from conflict | **63%** where members do it "a lot," vs. ≈14% where they don't |
| 2 | **Avoiding serious conflict.** The *strongest* predictor for Protestant churches | Protestant congregations in decline: **39%** with no conflict vs. **64%** with 4+ areas of serious conflict |
| 3 | **Pastor's time spent on evangelism and outreach** | **51%** ("a great deal") vs. **19%** ("little") |
| 4 | **Regular events aimed at the community** (non-members) | **44%** (monthly or more) vs. **18%** (never) |
| 5 | **Parenting or marriage-enrichment ministry** | **64%** where it's a specialty vs. **25%** with none |
| 6 | **Congregation sees itself as "spiritually vital and alive"** | ≈**47%** (strongly agree) vs. ≈**14%** (disagree) |
| 7 | **A clear sense of being distinct** from other nearby churches | Seeing yourself as "not that different" predicted *decline* |
| 8 | **Staffing.** Multiple full-time leaders | **46%** vs. ≈13–16% with a retired or absent leader (holds even controlling for size) |
| 9 | **Innovative worship scale** (projection, drums or electric guitar, self-described "innovative") | **56%** (highest score) vs. **18%** (lowest). Modest independent effect |

**Two findings with no independent effect:**
- **Websites, social media and number of technologies.** Use of them was correlated with growth (57% vs. 29%), but after controls there was *no independent effect* in 2010.
- **Theology.** Within mainline churches, liberal congregations grew at 22% and conservative ones at 19%. Within evangelical churches, it was 46% vs. 45%. Theological orientation was not what separated growers from decliners.

> Hadaway's own summary: "A congregation cannot presume that new people will somehow find their way to a congregation and eventually join. Recruitment efforts are essential for growth and the leader of the congregation must be involved."

The 2020 FACT overview (Thumma, 2021) re-ran this analysis on 15,278 congregations. It reports that "these factors that correlate with flourishing religious communities have not changed dramatically across the 20 years of our research."

## 2. Full table: FACTs on Growth 2010, one factor at a time

"% growing" means the share of congregations in the **highest growth category**: substantial net *and* percentage growth in average weekly worship attendance, 2005 to 2010. Across all congregations, roughly a third reached this tier.

### Things the congregation controls

| Factor (survey question) | Low end | High end | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Members involved in recruiting new members | ≈14% (not at all) | **63%** (a lot) | ~49 pts |
| Willing to change to meet new challenges | **17%** (disagree) | **48%** (strongly agree) | 31 pts |
| Has a clear mission and purpose | **16%** (disagree) | **45%** (strongly agree) | 29 pts |
| Pastor's time in evangelism or recruitment | **19%** (little) | **51%** (a great deal) | 32 pts |
| Pastor's time developing and promoting vision | ≈16% (little) | ≈48% (a great deal) | ~32 pts |
| Special events to attract the community, past year | **18%** (never) | **44%** (monthly or more) | 26 pts |
| Parenting or marriage enrichment activities | **25%** (none) | **64%** (specialty) | 39 pts |
| Young-adult ministry | n/a | **54%** (specialty) | n/a |
| Prayer or meditation groups | **21%** (none) | **47%** (specialty) | 26 pts |
| Ways visitors are followed up (mail, phone, email, visit, materials) | ≈27% (0–1 ways) | ≈43% (4–5 ways) | ~16 pts |
| Children or youth speak, read or perform in worship | ≈21% (never) | **45%** (always) | ~24 pts |
| Changed worship format or style in past 5 years | ≈29% (no change) | ≈47–49% (changed a lot / added a new service) | ~19 pts |
| Innovative worship scale | **18%** (lowest) | **56%** (highest) | 38 pts |
| Worship described as "joyful" | ≈20% (not / somewhat) | ≈44% (very well) | ~24 pts |
| Number of weekend worship services | **26%** (one) | **47–48%** (three or more) | ~21 pts |
| Congregation "wants more members" | **16%** (disagree) | **40%** (strongly agree) | 24 pts |
| Number of new technologies used | ≈29% (none) | **57%** (5–6) | 28 pts, but **no independent effect** |

### Context the congregation mostly can't control

| Factor | Low | High |
|---|---|---|
| Share of attendees aged 50+ | **16%** grow when 76%+ are 50+ | **50%** grow when ≤20% are 50+ |
| Share of attendees aged 34 or under | ≈21% (≤10% young) | ≈42% (41%+ young) |
| Region | **29%** (non-South) | **42%** (South) |
| Location | ≈29% (rural or small town) | ≈46% (downtown or central city) |
| Mainline vs. conservative Protestant | ≈18–22% (mainline) | ≈42–46% (conservative/evangelical) |

Hadaway's note on the "one service vs. several" result: growth and adding services feed each other. He says controlling for size "suggests that congregations tend to add worship services to accommodate additional attendees *and to encourage growth*."

## 3. What newcomers say drives their choice

### Pew Research Center, 2016: "Choosing a New Church or House of Worship"
Nationally representative. These are adults who have *ever looked* for a new congregation, which is **49%** of U.S. adults. Moving is the #1 trigger.

| Factor rated "important" in choosing | % |
|---|---|
| Quality of sermons | **83%** |
| Feeling welcomed by clergy and lay leaders | **79%** |
| Style of worship services | **74%** |
| Location | **70%** |
| Quality of religious education for children | **56%** (**65%** among parents of minors) |

| How they searched | % |
|---|---|
| Attended a service | **85%** |
| Talked to members of the congregation | **69%** |
| Talked to friends or colleagues | **68%** |
| Talked to the clergy | **55%** |
| Looked online | **37%** (**59%** of under-30s; **47%** of those who searched in the last 5 years) |

Also from Pew: **45%** of people who moved to their community in the last five years looked for a new congregation in that window. **52%** of mainline Protestants who searched considered switching denominations. **Newcomers to the area are the single largest "in-market" group.**

### Gallup, March 2017
1,526 U.S. adults who attend monthly or more. The question was what is a "major factor" in why they attend:

| Reason | All | Protestants |
|---|---|---|
| Sermons that teach about scripture | **76%** | 83% |
| Sermons that connect religion to your own life | **75%** | 80% |
| Spiritual programs for children and teens | **64%** | 68% |
| Community outreach and volunteer opportunities | **59%** | 61% |
| Dynamic, inspiring leaders | **54%** | 53% |
| Social activities to get to know people | **49%** | 49% |
| Good choir, praise band or music | **38%** | 44% |

Music and style rank **last**. Preaching content, children's programming and outreach rank first.

## 4. The invitation funnel

### What gets an unchurched person through the door
Lifeway Research and the Billy Graham Center, 2016. n = 2,000 unchurched Americans, ±2.7%.

| Method | Would be effective / would attend |
|---|---|
| **Personal invitation from a friend or family member** | **51%** |
| TV commercial | 23% |
| Postcard | 23% |
| Church member knocking on the door | 21% |
| Facebook ad | 18% |

| Type of event they'd attend if invited | % |
|---|---|
| Neighborhood safety meeting | **62%** |
| Community service event | **51%** |
| Sports or exercise program | **46%** |
| Concert | **45%** |
| Neighborhood get-together | **45%** |
| **Worship service** | **35%** |
| Recovery group | 25% |
| Seminar on a spiritual topic | 24% |

A personal invitation is roughly **2–3× as effective** as any impersonal channel. Unchurched people are **~1.8× more willing** to come to a community event than to a worship service. This lines up with Hadaway's finding that community events are an independent growth predictor.

### The Lutheran-specific gap
Lifeway Research, September 2023. n = 1,008 U.S. Protestant churchgoers, ±3.2%.

- Among all Protestant churchgoers, **60%** invited at least one person to church in the past 6 months, and **33%** invited no one.
- **Lutherans: 52% had invited no one**, among the highest of any denomination.
- **24%** of Lutherans said they don't invite because they aren't comfortable asking (Baptists: 9%).
- Among those 65 and older, **46%** had invited no one.
- **27%** of people who attend 4+ times a month made 3+ invitations, vs. **11%** of less frequent attenders.

Put that next to Hadaway's 14% → 63% recruiting gradient. For a typical Lutheran congregation, member invitation behavior is probably the **single largest controllable gap** in the data.

## 5. Baselines: what "normal" looks like right now

Use these to judge your own numbers.

| Benchmark | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Congregations that grew 5%+ in attendance, 2015–2020 | **34%** (down from 53% in 2000) | FACT 2020 |
| Congregations that grew 25%+, 2015–2020 | **23%** | FACT 2020 |
| Median 5-year attendance change, all congregations | **−7%** | FACT 2020 |
| Median 5-year change, **Mainline Protestant** (incl. ELCA) | **−12.5%** (median size 50) | FACT 2020 |
| Median 5-year change, Evangelical Protestant (FACT 2010 explicitly groups LCMS and WELS here) | **−5.4%** (median size 65) | FACT 2020 |
| Median congregation size | 137 (2000) → **65** (2020) → **60 in person / 75 incl. virtual** (2023) | FACT 2020; EPIC 2023 |
| Attendees aged 65+ | **33%** in 2020 → **36%** in 2023 (U.S. adults: 17%). **Mainline: ~50%** | FACT 2020; EPIC 2023 |
| Share of attendees who are new since 2020 | **16%**, consistent with the long-run norm of **~5% new attenders per year** | EPIC 2023 (n = 4,809 churches) |
| Attenders who joined their current church in the past 5 years | **38%** | EPIC 2024–25 (24,000+ attenders) |
| Churches above pre-pandemic attendance (in person + online) | **33%** | EPIC 2023 |
| Churches offering hybrid (in person + online) worship | **73%** (vs. 20% streaming in 2019) | EPIC 2023 |
| Multiracial congregations that grew 5%+ vs. majority-white | ≈**49%** vs. ≈**32%** | FACT 2020, Fig. 20 |

"Typical" isn't standing still. Even a stable church loses people to moves and deaths and replaces them with **~5% new attenders per year**. Growth means beating that replacement rate.

## 6. Practical KPIs with research-backed targets

These turn the findings above into things a congregation can actually count:

| KPI | How to measure | Benchmark or target from the data |
|---|---|---|
| **Invitation rate** | Quick anonymous poll: "Have you invited anyone to worship or an event in the last 6 months?" | Protestant avg **60%**; Lutherans **≤48%** (52% invited no one). Growth tier = members involved "a lot" |
| **Community-facing events** | Count events designed for non-members | **≥1 per month** (44% top-tier growth vs. 18% for never) |
| **Visitor follow-up channels** | Number of distinct contacts per first-time guest (card, call, email, visit, mailed material) | **3+ channels**; best results at 4–5 |
| **New attenders per year ÷ avg attendance** | Track first-time guests who become regulars | Long-run norm **~5%/yr**; above that = growth |
| **Age mix** | % of attendees aged 50+ and aged ≤34 | Growth odds fall sharply once **>50%** are 50+ (16–25% growing) |
| **Pastor time on outreach** | Hours per week in evangelism, community presence or recruitment | "Quite a bit" or "a great deal" ↔ 42–51% growing |
| **Children or youth roles in worship** | Share of services where kids read, speak or perform | "Always" ↔ 45% growing vs. ≈21% for never |
| **Serious conflict** | Count of areas of serious conflict in past 5 years | **0**. Each added area raises decline risk (39% → 64%) |
| **Online presence** | Online and hybrid attendance tracked separately (ELCA Form A already asks for this) | Necessary for discovery (**59%** of under-30 searchers look online) but not an independent growth driver on its own |

## 7. Caveats

1. **These are correlations, not experiments.** No randomized trial of "church growth tactics" exists. FACT is a key-informant survey, usually filled out by the pastor, so items like "spiritually vital" and "clear mission" are self-assessed. Hadaway's multivariate controls help but can't prove cause and effect.
2. **Reverse causation is real for some items.** Growing churches *add* services and staff; they don't only grow *because of* them. Hadaway flags this himself.
3. **The 2010 multivariate analysis is the most rigorous public one, but it's pre-pandemic.** FACT 2020 says the same correlates hold. EPIC 2023–25 data shows the overall growth/decline split (~⅓ growing, ~½ declining) hasn't changed.
4. **Denominational family matters, and it can't be changed.** ELCA congregations sit in the "mainline" group, which has lower base rates, older members and more decline. LCMS and WELS are in the "conservative Protestant" group. The *levers* (invitation, events, follow-up, conflict avoidance, family ministry) worked within every family Hadaway examined.
5. **≈ values** were read from bar charts in the original PDFs. Text-stated values have no ≈.
